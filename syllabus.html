Catalog Description

This course provides a practical approach to the study of digital and integrated devices. Topics include logic gates, flip-flops, latches, counters, encoders/decoders, multiplexers, arithmetic circuits, oscillators, timers, analog-to-digital circuits, digital-to-analog circuits, solid-state memory, and operational amplifiers. The study of solid-state circuits and devices covers diodes, transistors, FETs, thyristors, and optoelectronic devices. Common circuits include power supplies, amplifiers, solid-state switches, and regulators.

VI. Classroom Policies & Procedures

▾
⚠️
A student who does not officially withdraw may receive a grade of 'F' depending on course progress or attendance. Dates: Drop with refund — Jan 24, 2025; Drop with 'W' — Jan 31, 2025.
📅 Attendance Policy
For classes at the school, you are expected to be on time and attend all classes. There is a 5-point penalty for missing class and a 1-point penalty for being late — both deducted from participation points.
🔬 Lab Policy
Attend all lab sessions and participate in lab activities. No word-for-word collaboration on lab discussion questions and conclusions — each student must write their own answers, otherwise only half the points are awarded. Team assignments are the exception.
📝 Exam Policy
Take all weekly exams by due dates. There will be no test make-ups without prior arrangements. The Final Exam will be online — you may use your notes and textbook.
🔄 Make-Up Policy
Chapter Review Questions and quizzes may be completed after the due date with a penalty of 10% per day. If you miss class, hands-on labs must be completed before moving on to labs for the next module.
⭐ Extra Credit
Extra credit is not provided in this course.
🛡 Academic Integrity / Cheating & Plagiarism
The CIT Department upholds MVCC's Code of Academic Integrity. Students are expected to complete all work reflecting professional ethics and standards.

Penalties: A first offense results in a grade of "0" or "F" for the work with no opportunity to redo it. A second offense results in an "F" for the course, and a report is submitted to the Dean of Science, Business and Computer Technology.

Cheating includes unauthorized assistance, information sharing, and use of cloud services to store/share academic materials. Plagiarism includes submitting another's work — or AI-generated work — as your own without permission or citation.
🏥 Safety / Impaired Concentration
Students with impaired ability to concentrate may risk jeopardizing safety in the lab. Discuss any condition that could impair concentration with the instructor prior to operating equipment or performing laboratory procedures. Failure to notify the instructor may violate the Code of Student Conduct.
🏛 Testing Center
The Testing Center will not be used in this course.
